## Key findings

- No Nigerian rate card for same-day intra-Lagos delivery was found. The only price ranges come from one agency blog that calls them "order of magnitude" estimates: NGN 1,500 to 3,500 (USD 1.13 to 2.63) per run, NGN 2,000 to 3,000 (USD 1.50 to 2.25) on the Lekki corridor [S](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/same-day-delivery-zones-and-cost-lagos-2026). Treat as weak; the founder's own quotes from five riders or couriers are needed.
- Small social sellers are a real, documented segment with a delivery problem: they often deliver themselves or use small, inefficient logistics firms [E] (TechCabal 2022 search summary; snippet only, page not opened for that passage). No survey sizing the segment was found.
- Sellers do pay up and switch for delivery when it matters, but the evidence is about interstate parcels, not same-day intra-city: sellers use options at NGN 5,000 to 7,500 (USD 3.76 to 5.63) per parcel, and some lose customers when delivery costs rise [S](https://techcabal.com/2024/03/16/nigerian-businesses-are-forced-to-navigate-high-flying-delivery-prices/) (March 2024, before later naira moves).
- Cost to win one paying seller is wide: NGN 2,500 to 100,000 (USD 1.88 to 75.13) [E] (method below). The top of the range matters most: if few leads convert, paid ads do not work and the founders must sell in person and through referrals.
- Instagram reach is modest in the target audience: about 3.7% of Nigeria's population were addressable Instagram users in September 2026 [S](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/instagram-users-in-nigeria/2026/06/). WhatsApp share was not verified.
- No waiting lists, search-trend data or complaint threads about same-day delivery for sellers were found. Demand evidence is indirect.

## Evidence

### 1. Who buys

Paying customer and user are the same person: the small online seller (fashion, beauty, gadgets, food items) selling through Instagram, WhatsApp and small sites, per the brief. Their buyers sometimes bear the fee.

- Social commerce is large across Africa: a 2019 GeoPoll survey found Facebook and Instagram used more than marketplaces for online shopping [S](https://techcabal.com/2022/04/07/catlog-wants-to-improve-social-commerce-in-nigeria/). The figure is 7 years old and not Lagos-specific.
- Catlog, a Lagos seller tool, reported "hundreds" of Nigerian sellers in April 2022 [S](https://techcabal.com/2022/04/07/catlog-wants-to-improve-social-commerce-in-nigeria/). Old; shows sellers adopt tools.
- Number of sellers sending 5 to 50 parcels a week in Lagos Island and Lekki: unknown. No source.
- Instagram addressable users in Nigeria: 3.7% of the population, Sept 2026, "addressable audience" estimate only [S](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/instagram-users-in-nigeria/2026/06/).

### 2. How often

- Brief states 5 to 50 parcels a week per seller [A] (founder's target definition, not validated). Weekly fee spend at NGN 2,000 (USD 1.50) per parcel would be NGN 10,000 to 100,000 (USD 7.51 to 75.13) a week [E] (5 to 50 parcels x NGN 2,000, price taken as the midpoint of the weak blog range).
- Drivers: order volume from Instagram and WhatsApp posts, promotion days. Seasonality not researched in this lane.

### 3. Current alternative

- Independent riders found by WhatsApp, courier firms, or sellers delivering themselves or using friends (brief; self-delivery supported by the TechCabal 2022 passage, [E] snippet only, page not opened for that passage).
- Cost: NGN 1,500 to 2,500 (USD 1.13 to 1.88) on the Island, NGN 2,000 to 3,000 (USD 1.50 to 2.25) on the Lekki corridor, NGN 2,500 to 3,500 (USD 1.88 to 2.63) for Ajah/outer [S](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/same-day-delivery-zones-and-cost-lagos-2026), 23 August 2026. This is a Senegal-based web agency blog. Its other posts give different numbers (a search summary showed NGN 3,000 to 4,500 for Lekki/Ajah, and NGN 1,800 to 2,600 elsewhere), so the range is NGN 1,500 to 4,500 (USD 1.13 to 3.38) [E] (low and high of the blog posts seen; two posts only seen in search summary).
- First-attempt success 70 to 85% by zone, per the same blog [S](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/same-day-delivery-zones-and-cost-lagos-2026). Not independently checked.
- Risk to sellers: cash-on-delivery complicates cash flow for small sellers [E] (DHL Nigeria article, snippet only, page not opened). Some Instagram sellers avoid it by requiring payment first [E] (Techpoint, snippet only, page not opened).
- What would make them switch: reliability, cash remittance, tracking. What makes switching hard: sellers already have a known rider and trust is personal. These are the analyst's reading [A] (no direct evidence found), to be tested in interviews.

### 4. Willingness to pay

- Observed prices sellers or buyers accepted (interstate, 2024): NGN 5,000 (USD 3.76) station drop, NGN 7,500 (USD 5.63) doorstep, NGN 18,000 (USD 13.52) DHL next-day; a regional service at NGN 6,000 to 7,000 (USD 4.51 to 5.26) [S](https://techcabal.com/2024/03/16/nigerian-businesses-are-forced-to-navigate-high-flying-delivery-prices/). One seller lost customers after DHL prices roughly doubled. Different product from same-day intra-city; shows price sensitivity, not WTP for this offer.
- Same-day uplift in conversion of 12 to 18% is claimed by the same agency [S](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/same-day-delivery-zones-and-cost-lagos-2026) as an order of magnitude with no method. Weak; do not rely on it.
- Rider cost floor: Glovo riders earn NGN 700 to 2,000 (USD 0.53 to 1.50) per order depending on distance, self-reported [S](https://nairametrics.com/2026/05/30/inside-lagos-delivery-boom-riders-risking-everything-to-survive/) (30 May 2026). A same-day fee below about NGN 1,500 is unlikely to cover rider pay plus the app's costs [A] (analyst inference, margin not modelled here).
- Stated intent from sellers: none found. A COD-remittance fee has no local price evidence: unknown.

### 5. Reachable channels

- Instagram and WhatsApp (where the sellers are): organic posts and DMs to sellers, paid Meta ads. Nigerian Meta ad costs: CPM NGN 450 to 5,000 (USD 0.34 to 3.76), CPC NGN 30 to 300 (USD 0.02 to 0.23), cost per lead NGN 500 to 5,000 (USD 0.38 to 3.76); Meta adds 7.5% VAT [S](https://grey.co/blog/facebook-ads-cost-nigeria) (Grey, published 28 Aug 2026; a fintech marketing blog, not Meta data). An independent aggregator gave CPM about USD 1.50 and CPC USD 0.12 [E] (Adamigo, snippet only, page not opened). The two sources agree on CPM, differ on CPC.
- Test budget advised by Grey: NGN 5,000 to 10,000 (USD 3.76 to 7.51) a day [S](https://grey.co/blog/facebook-ads-cost-nigeria). Below NGN 60,000 (USD 45.08) a month the algorithm is said not to learn.
- Direct and referral channels: visiting sellers at Lekki, Ajah, Island markets and fashion/beauty pop-ups; rider-to-seller referral; seller communities. No cost data found [A] (no source; to be tested).
- Partner platforms: seller tools such as Catlog already hold seller lists [S](https://techcabal.com/2022/04/07/catlog-wants-to-improve-social-commerce-in-nigeria/). Partnership terms unknown.

### 6. Cost to acquire a paying customer

Method (all inputs labelled):

- Cost per lead (a seller enquiry via ad): NGN 500 to 5,000 (USD 0.38 to 3.76) [S](https://grey.co/blog/facebook-ads-cost-nigeria).
- Lead to paying seller (completes at least one paid parcel): 5% to 20% [A] (no local figure; a B2B-style onboarding of small sellers, guess to be replaced by pilot data).
- Low end: NGN 500 / 20% = NGN 2,500 (USD 1.88) [E]. High end: NGN 5,000 / 5% = NGN 100,000 (USD 75.13) [E].
- Realistic planning band for Moderate case: NGN 15,000 to 40,000 (USD 11.27 to 30.05) [A] (middle of the range plus about NGN 5,000 to 10,000 per seller for a referral reward or a rider's onboarding visit, both assumed).
- Compare: first-year fee revenue from a seller sending 5 parcels a week at NGN 2,000 is about NGN 520,000 (USD 390.68) [E] (5 x 52 x NGN 2,000), before rider cost and churn. Rider cost and margin belong to the model lane.

### 7. Repeat behaviour

- No local retention data for seller delivery. Delivery is a repeat operational need, so repeat is likely if service is reliable [A] (reasoning, no evidence).
- Comparison inside Nigeria, different product: Chowdeck reports more than 10 million deliveries and over 2.1 million users [S](https://nairametrics.com/2026/05/30/inside-lagos-delivery-boom-riders-risking-everything-to-survive/), about 4.8 deliveries per user over its life [E] (10 million / 2.1 million). Food consumers, not sellers; do not read as seller retention.
- Seller switching evidence: sellers dropped DHL and tried night buses, waybill and other couriers when price or reliability changed [S](https://techcabal.com/2024/03/16/nigerian-businesses-are-forced-to-navigate-high-flying-delivery-prices/). Sellers do not stay loyal to a courier that fails them; this cuts both ways.

### 8. Demand signals

- Complaints about current options: late delivery, rude drivers, hassle coordinating riders, price spikes [S](https://techcabal.com/2024/03/16/nigerian-businesses-are-forced-to-navigate-high-flying-delivery-prices/). One seller: "it was such a hassle coordinating the entire process, and so I gave up" (about interstate waybill, not Lagos same-day).
- Delivery market is active: Glovo Nigeria delivered about 38 million items in 2025 [S](https://nairametrics.com/2026/05/30/inside-lagos-delivery-boom-riders-risking-everything-to-survive/) (company statement, food and items, not parcels).
- Not found: search-trend data, waiting lists, WhatsApp group posts about same-day parcel delivery for sellers. Say plainly: no direct demand signal for this exact offer.
