# Region pack: Sub-Saharan Africa

Typically covers: Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, South Africa, Tanzania, Uganda, Ethiopia, Rwanda, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire and neighbours. The founder's launch country is the primary market. Use neighbouring countries only as comparison or as expansion cases, and say so when you do.

This pack is a checklist of questions, not a set of facts. Answer each item with a source and an evidence label (`[S]`, `[E]` or `[A]`) as set out in `<PLUGIN_ROOT>/skills/before-you-build/references/conventions.md`. Check everything live. If an item does not apply to the launch country, say so in one line with a source and move on. If no source can be found, say so and label the answer `[A]` with the reason.

## Common checks

1. Which payment methods do people in the launch country actually use for this kind of purchase, and what fees does each charge the seller?
2. How common is cash-on-delivery for this product category, and what does it do to refusals, returns and cash collection?
3. Which logistics providers serve the launch cities and the regions beyond them, and what are their delivery cost, failure rate and return rate for this kind of parcel or service?
4. What does the festival calendar and seasonality look like for this category, including public holidays and shopping campaigns, and when do demand, delivery capacity and ad costs peak or drop?
5. What business registration is needed, how is tax applied to this revenue model, and which consumer-protection and data rules apply?
6. What is the funding climate for this kind of company in the last 12 to 24 months: which investors are active, which deals closed, and which have stopped?
7. Who are the dominant local and regional competitors, and what do they do that a newcomer must match?
8. How do people here decide whom to trust before buying this kind of product (reviews, referrals, seller reputation on social platforms, a known brand, payment on delivery)?

## Region-specific checks

- Which mobile money services and agent networks are used, who are the operators and regulators, and what are the fees, transaction limits and interoperability across operators and borders?
- Does the launch country behave like a mobile-money market or a card-based one for this kind of purchase, and what does that mean for how customers pay?
- Is the local currency freely convertible, and what are the rules, delays and costs for repatriating profits, paying foreign suppliers and receiving investor money?
- How reliable are power and mobile connectivity in the launch locations, and what does an outage or a data-cost barrier do to this product?
- How large is the informal market for this category (open-air markets, street traders, unregistered suppliers), and how does a formal player compete on price and trust?
- How do customers find and verify addresses, and what do logistics providers do about missing street addresses, poor roads and cash handling?
- What regulatory or licensing hurdles apply to this product (payments, telecoms, health, agriculture, import), and how consistent is enforcement between countries and regions of the same country?
- How do development-finance institutions, grants and diaspora investors shape funding here, and what do they expect (impact reporting, currency of the round)?
- How different is the launch country from its neighbours in language, payment rails and regulation, so that expansion means a fresh launch?

## Local-language search terms

Search in English and in French (West and Central Africa), Portuguese (Angola, Mozambique) or Swahili (East Africa), and check for widely used local languages and pidgin where customers talk online. Local tech press, central-bank notices and mobile-operator websites are the best sources.

Example search patterns (replace the words in angle brackets with the idea's own terms, translated):

- `<product> price in <city> (English and local language)`
- `<central bank> mobile money / payments guidelines latest`
- `<product> prix à <city> / bei ya <product> (price, local word)`
- `<country> startup funding round this year`
- `<product> delivery problems complaints <city> (social media groups)`
